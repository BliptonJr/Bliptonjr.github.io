"If we throw it overboard, they'll stop chasing us." Nobody answered immediately. Millions of people on their homeworld desperately needed the substance. It could transform their civilization for generations. Yet every ship pursuing them risked destruction if the chase continued. Finally, Stivio spoke. "We finish the mission." They swallowed hard. "But nobody dies for it if we can help it." As the Courier entered the nebula, Bonji suddenly closed their eyes. They ignored every instrument on the bridge. Instead, they listened. Not with their ears. With the hull beneath their feet. Years spent flying the aging ship had taught Bonji something every sensor had missed. The vibrations were repeating. The turbulence wasn't random. It followed a pattern. KanRita instantly recognized what Bonji had discovered. Overlaying Bonji's instinctive observations onto their mental star maps revealed invisible gravitational standing waves hidden inside the nebula. The realization struck them both simultaneously. Bonji's stubborn refusal to dismiss the ship's subtle warnings—something others often considered superstition—combined perfectly with KanRita's relentless determination to redeem a past navigational mistake. Alone, neither could have solved the problem. Together, they saw a path no computer could calculate. Execution demanded absolute trust. Bonji ignored every navigation display and flew entirely by feel, sensing tiny changes in the hull's vibrations seconds before each gravitational shift occurred. KanRita counted corrections aloud. "Left three degrees." Bonji adjusted. "Roll." The ship rolled. "Dive." The Courier plunged into an invisible corridor between colliding plasma streams. "Full burn." The engines roared. Behind them, MAR relied on sophisticated computers that interpreted the nebula as random chaos. The pirates trusted speed instead of precision. Both were wrong. The corporate formation fractured as gravitational shear twisted ships apart from one another. Pirate vessels disappeared into violent electromagnetic storms after chasing false sensor echoes. The Starlight Courier escaped not because of luck or superior firepower, but because two flawed people trusted instincts and experience that no machine could replicate. Days later, battered but intact, they delivered the cargo safely to their homeworld. The ship was saved. The debt was paid. Neb abandoned any thought of stealing the substance. Stivio finally stopped checking the crate every five minutes. KanRita found redemption. Bonji quietly polished the old brass compass one last time. Weeks later, engineers dismantled the ancient tracking transmitter that had betrayed them. Hidden inside its encrypted firmware they discovered something impossible. The tracking protocol had not been written by Meridian Asset Recovery. It hadn't been written by the shipyard. It wasn't written in any programming language recognized by modern civilization. Embedded at the very end of the code was a single unfamiliar symbol. It matched the same mysterious engraving found on the container that had once held Liquid Common Sense. Someone—or something—had planned this journey long before the Starlight Courier was ever built. The greatest mystery had never been who betrayed them. It was who had quietly been guiding them all along. The End… for now.
